A first difficulty arises from the undeniable fact that, contrary to his false statement, my opponent Mr. Rumsey never made any transfer of funds , either to me or to any member of my family, either in euros or in any other currency, and that, consequently, he was never able to produce any proof of such a transfer, which, moreover, the courts never required of him. A careful examination of the copy of the bank transfer order in the file reveals in reality a completely different banking transaction and a completely different originator.

Under these conditions, can we imagine the reimbursement of funds to a complainant who never made any transfer of these funds?

A second difficulty lies in the inescapable reality that this case undeniably involves not only a partner of my opponent, named Rustin Howard, but also a company of which both were and still are among the principal shareholders. It was Mr. Howard, and not Mr. Rumsey, who was the author of the banking transaction, by way of a normal and customary advance on wages that he made to me in my capacity as a contractual employee of precisely this same company, of which he happened to be both the president and the treasurer, and, as such, had been in charge since 2005 of the contractual and obligatory payment of all my wages.

Therefore, neither the company nor Mr. Howard can be legally separated from the Rumsey case.

Furthermore, it appears that this company owes me, with respect to unpaid salaries and the reimbursement of operating advances since January 2005, amounts considerably higher than the sum which is the subject of this hearing.

Thus, even assuming the legitimacy of the reimbursement of a salary incontestably due under the terms of the law but which a fictitious loan attempts to fraudulently replace, this reimbursement could only be credited exclusively to the entity which paid me the sum , and as a deduction from the debt owed to me.

Furthermore, from an accounting point of view, it should be noted that an income net of costs much higher than the amount of the bank transfer had been provided by me to the company within three months of the transfer order, that is to say more than ten years ago, precisely to provide for the payment of salaries, which were moreover and in any case legally guaranteed on Mr Howard's own assets, but which the fictitious loan fraudulently attempted to replace.

The facts and sums I state are accompanied by irrefutable written evidence, which I provide again at the bottom of this document, and I ardently hope that the latter will finally be consulted in detail with all the attention they deserve.

 

   II.        INTRODUCTION

This conciliation hearing concerns the payment to an American citizen and resident, David Rumsey, of more than three times the sum of 38,250 euros (or 38,750 according to certain documents ) which he falsely claims to have given me on June 4, 2013 but which he never gave me.

A careful reading of the bank transfer order that was included in the file reveals, in a manner that could not be clearer, that the author was in no way Mr. Rumsey, that the currency was not the European currency, and that the principal was none other than the employer who commonly and habitually paid me my contractual salaries, especially since he had a legal obligation to pay them to me and, moreover, had formally guaranteed them on his own assets.

I repeat that, beyond doubt, my opponent, Mr. David Rumsey, never paid me any sum of any kind, neither in euros nor in any other currency, neither to me nor to any member of my family, neither on 4 June 2013 nor on any other date, and, as a result, would obviously never have been able to produce any tangible proof of his alleged payment, which, moreover, the courts have never demanded of him.

On the other hand, a sum of 50,000 US dollars was paid to me on June 4, 2013 by the American citizen and resident Rustin Howard, who happens to be the president of the American company DeepGulf, which employed me at the time as Managing Director and Principal Engineer. This payment was clearly an advance on the salaries that Mr. Howard irrevocably owed me, not only for contractual and legal reasons, but also by virtue of the obligations imposed by the American government, relating to the granting of my visas, especially since Mr. Howard had formally committed with the American government to guarantee the entirety of my salaries, if necessary from his own assets.

For the record, the exchange rate is currently around 0.95 euros for one US dollar.

This payment was not out of the ordinary, since in previous years Mr. Howard had made to my credit a total of 64 (sixty-four) bank transfers relating to the payment of my salaries, the last of which, for 50,000 US dollars, is fraudulently the subject of this case, this amount representing approximately 7.25% of the total payments made to my credit by Mr. Howard.

Mr. Howard and Mr. Rumsey were long-standing friends and business partners, and, in particular, both were and still are among the principal shareholders of the DeepGulf company which employed me and therefore owed me my wages, particularly as I was its sole generator of revenue.

To claim, as Mr. Rumsey does, that this matter is completely separate from the DeepGulf company and the salaries owed to me therefore amounts to fraudulent manipulation, since Mr. Rumsey never made any payment to me, whereas the transfer order produced in the file concerns an advance on salary made by my employer Mr. Howard in a transaction of which he was very familiar, which the company's accounts indicate very clearly.

It also turns out that between 30 July and 9 September 2013, that is, within three months of the advance payment, I had proceeded to DeepGulf the amount of $66,250, not including the payment of an additional sum of $26,250 in the months that followed, in order to provide for the payment of salaries, which means, without any doubt, that an amount well in excess of the sum of $50,000 had been contributed by me within a very short period of time more than ten years ago.

In addition, this same company, DeepGulf, owes me very large amounts concerning not only the unpaid salaries that were contractually owed to me, but also the very large sums that I had personally made available to the company for its operation prior to the first contracts of 2008.

Excluding interest, the company now owes me the sum of $1,190,500.

Applying the same 11.45% interest rate that the Court awarded Mr. Rumsey in that case, the company now owes me $5,454,344.

 

  III.        DEDUCTION AND REIMBURSEMENT

Although, according to French and American case law prohibiting unjust enrichment, a sum can only be owed exclusively to an entity that actually paid it, and not to an entity that never disbursed it, in the specific case of this affair not only did this sum represent a simple advance on a legally and contractually acquired salary, but also a provision much higher than the original payment of $50,000 had been very quickly credited by me to the company's accounts, and, moreover, the reimbursement requested paled in comparison to the company's debt to me. In the worst case, the hypothetical claim could only be deducted from the debt to the credit of my company current account, after which the company, of which Mr. Rumsey is a principal shareholder, would remain liable to me for the sum of $5,454,344 which is still unquestionably owed to me, the details of the transaction being set out in paragraph 27 of the following history.
